# Cut shorter, link harder, post slightly more

Lolcow Cinema should change *what* its Shorts are much more than *how many* it posts. The evidence from 2024-2026 supports moving most Shorts from today's 90-179 s cuts to 15-60 s hot-take cuts, wiring every Short to the long segment it came from, and raising volume only modestly and only after measuring. It does not support "many more Shorts" as the main route to long-form growth. At 3 a day the channel already posts about 90 Shorts a month, well past the volumes where third-party data shows the long-form benefit of Shorts disappearing (35+ a month) and entertainment-channel revenue per view collapsing (21+ a month). YouTube says it does not penalize volume and that Shorts "can help drive traffic" to long videos, but it has published no conversion numbers, and the best independent figures put Shorts-to-long-form click-through well under 1%. Two hard constraints shape the answer for a clip channel. Any Short over 60 s with an active Content ID claim of any kind is blocked worldwide. And YouTube's reused-content and "inauthentic content" policies look hardest at exactly the profile an automated, high-volume clip pipeline produces. The pipeline already holds most of the fix: scoring marks a 15-60 s "short window" in every clip that nothing uses yet.

## Shorts reach new viewers, but convert to long-form at well under 1%

YouTube's official position, from its May 2025 myths post, is that Shorts are "a powerful tool for reaching new and diverse viewers," that they "can help drive traffic to your long-form content," and that it has "not seen evidence" of Shorts hurting long-form, because it "built separate recommendation systems optimized for each" format ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/debunking-common-myths-about-youtube-shorts/)). The post contains **no statistics**. YouTube's Help pages add that what a viewer discovers about topics or channels informs recommendations across formats ([YouTube Help 16559651](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559651)). That is the real mechanism: a Shorts viewer becomes a long-form viewer through topic and channel signals and explicit links, not automatically. "No evidence of harm" is a weaker claim than "Shorts drive long-form."

Independent data is mixed and correlational. The only before/after study, a non-peer-reviewed arXiv preprint of 250 creators over 100K subscribers, found **mean long-form views fell by about 744,000 per channel** after creators started Shorts, with the drop concentrated in long-form-focused channels. It had no control group ([arXiv 2402.18208](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18208)). Metricool's 2026 study of about 800,000 videos found long-form videos averaged **8,789 views on channels posting about 5 Shorts a month**, 46% above the 5,985 average, while channels posting **35+ Shorts a month** were "roughly the same level as channels posting none" ([Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/)). AIR Media-Tech's study of 18,000 channels found the best Shorts share is **25-40% of uploads**, with long-form performance dropping above about 55% Shorts ([AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/do-youtube-shorts-help-your-long-form-videos-grow-data-from-18000-channels)). For subscribers specifically, the niche matters. In AIR's data, mixing in Shorts beat long-only by 31-41% in fitness, business, education and food, but in **entertainment** the two were tied at small scale (72,400 vs 73,600 median subscribers at 100K-1M) and long-only led at larger scale (214,000 vs 183,000) ([AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/do-youtube-shorts-help-your-long-form-videos-grow-data-from-18000-channels)). An older study by Paddy Galloway found Shorts converted **16.9 subscribers per 10,000 views against 22.7 for long-form** ([The Publish Press](https://news.thepublishpress.com/p/are-shorts-worth-it)). That study predates YouTube's March 2025 change to how Shorts views are counted, so its ratios do not carry over directly.

The conversion figure that matters most here is also the shakiest. AIR treats raising Shorts-to-long-form conversion from **0.04% to 0.5%** as "worth more than doubling your upload frequency," but gives no method ([AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/do-youtube-shorts-help-your-long-form-videos-grow-data-from-18000-channels)). Taken at face value, 10,000 Shorts views send between 4 and 50 people to a long video. The spread between those two numbers comes from *how* a Short links, not how many Shorts there are. The claim that Shorts and long-form audiences overlap only about 10% is widely repeated but comes from no named source ([Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-hurting-long-form-views)). The honest reading is that for an entertainment clip channel, Shorts are a reliable reach engine and an unreliable funnel. Their value to the channel depends on whether the channel's own analytics show Shorts viewers arriving at segments.

The money points the same way. Creators keep 45% of their share of a pooled Shorts ad pot ([YouTube Help 12504220](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220)). AIR's 2026 data puts Shorts RPM at **$0.07-0.20 per 1,000 views in most niches, 3-14% of long-form**, so matching 1,000 long-form views takes 11,000-26,000 Shorts views. Entertainment channels saw blended RPM fall from **$4.99 at zero Shorts a month to $1.15 at 21+** ([AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/youtube-shorts-rpm-vs-long-form-how-much-do-shorts-earn-in-2026)). AIR says causation is unproven, but its own conclusion is that the economic value of Shorts lies in converting viewers to long-form. Shorts also do little for Partner Program eligibility on this channel. Shorts-feed watch time does not count toward the 4,000 long-form hours, and the Shorts route requires **10M Shorts views in 90 days** ([YouTube Help 72851](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851)). Long segments are the realistic path in, and Shorts earn their keep by feeding them.

## The 90-179 s window sits in the weakest and riskiest length band

The largest length dataset found, from quso.ai (108,138 Shorts, live at least 14 days), shows median views peaking in short cuts and collapsing past 90 seconds:

| Length | Median views | vs 11-20 s peak |
|---|---|---|
| 0-10 s | 514 | 57% |
| 11-20 s | **901** | 100% |
| 21-30 s | 542 | 60% |
| 31-45 s | 347 | 39% |
| 46-60 s | 188 | 21% |
| 61-90 s | 186 | 21% |
| 90 s+ | **51** | 6% |

Source: [quso.ai](https://quso.ai/research/youtube-shorts-length). The data is correlational and comes from a vendor that sells clipping tools. A widely cited "Inflow Network" study claims the opposite (50-60 s beating under-10 s), but its original could not be found ([Piktochart summary](https://piktochart.com/blog/how-long-youtube-shorts/)). YouTube says there is no best length. When 3-minute Shorts launched, its Creator Insider account said the system "might take a bit to learn how to best serve them" alongside 15-60 s Shorts ([Creator Insider on X](https://twitter.com/YouTubeInsider/status/1844362998292394107)). The signals are consistent enough to act on, though. Every Short the pipeline currently makes lands in the bottom bucket. Inside the curve, 46-90 s is nearly flat, so a moment that genuinely needs a minute costs little extra. The cliff is past 90 s.

The rule that matters most for this channel is official and concrete. **"Any Short that is over one minute in duration with an active Content ID claim of any type, including manual claims, will be blocked globally"**: not playable, not recommended, not monetized. Shorts of 60 s or less see "no changes in limitations" ([YouTube Help 15424877](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)). Even a monetize claim, which would be harmless on a long video, kills a 61 s Short. Kino Casino and Channel 404 streams regularly play other people's video and music. The pipeline's `allow_reactions` setting permits up to 5 s of source audio in a Short. Whether 5 s is enough to trigger a match is not established in the research, and no data was found on how often stream-clip Shorts get claimed. But the asymmetry is stark. A Short of 60 s or less survives a claim; a 90-179 s Short does not. Any Short that contains source audio should be capped at 60 s.

One counterweight to read correctly: since 31 March 2025, a public Shorts "view" counts every start and replay, with no minimum watch time. The old intent-based count survives as "engaged views," which revenue and YPP use ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted)). Very short Shorts loop and so inflate public views mechanically. Length comparisons on this channel have to use engaged views, or 12 s cuts will look better than they are.

## Hot takes earn shares, but the policy risks are channel-wide

The case for building Shorts around hot takes is real but borrowed. The strongest evidence comes from text posts, not video. A PNAS study of Facebook and Twitter found each word referring to a political out-group raised shares by **about 67%**, roughly 4.8 times the effect of negative words ([Rathje et al., PNAS 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/8256037)). No YouTube-specific study shows opinionated Shorts beating neutral ones. The editing logic that pairs with hot takes is also mostly practitioner consensus: viewers decide in about 2 seconds, so cut greetings and build-up and open on the strongest line ([Choppity](https://www.choppity.com/blog/podcast-clips-for-social-media/)); keep captions large and short so the idea works on mute ([quso.ai](https://quso.ai/blog/how-to-get-more-views-on-shorts)); and end on a frame that hands back into the opening so the clip loops ([Deeside](https://www.deeside.com/the-shorts-recommendation-loop-a-practical-map-of-signals-you-can-actually-influence/)). YouTube's own Shorts lead stresses a hook in the first second and Shorts that work as self-contained "bits" ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/)). The one feed signal YouTube exposes to creators is "viewed vs swiped away," decided in those opening moments ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/)). No controlled study measures how much a cold open, captions or a loop adds. The "5-10x" figures in vendor blogs are undocumented. For stream clips the practical pattern is to start on the claim itself and put any needed context in an on-screen line ("Andy on X:") instead of ten seconds of preamble. Starting mid-sentence is fine when the first words are the provocative ones.

The constraints arrive in three places. First, YouTube's harassment policy covers insults aimed at identifiable people, bans malicious insults based on protected attributes, and can act on **a pattern of repeated targeting across several uploads even if no single video crosses the line** ([YouTube Help: Harassment](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2802268?hl=en)). That last clause matters for a channel named Lolcow Cinema that clips drama streams: a run of Shorts mocking the same person can add up to a violation. Second, profanity in titles and thumbnails still blocks full monetization, even though since 2025 spoken profanity in the first seconds no longer does ([YouTube Help: advertiser-friendly update log](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9725604?hl=en)). Third, and most serious, is the reused-content policy. It explicitly excludes "clips from a show edited together with little narrative" and content without substantive changes. It allows clips with commentary, a storyline, or a "meaningful difference" from the original, and it **applies to the channel as a whole and even with the original creator's permission** ([YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)). The July 2025 rename of "repetitious" to "inauthentic content" targets mass-produced, templated output with minimal variation ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-targeting-spam-not-reaction-channels/550755/)). YouTube said that update was not aimed at reaction or clip channels. Large streamer-clip channels operate at scale without recorded enforcement, which shows the model can survive, not that it is safe ([CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creator-news/the-rise-of-the-clip-economy/)). Don's permission protects against copyright claims, not against a reused-content ruling. More automated Shorts means more evidence for a reviewer to weigh when the channel applies to YPP. Per-Short editorial value (selection, framing text, captions, layout switches, varied titles) protects the channel. Raw count does not.

## Volume helps totals, not per-Short reach, and the cap binds first

YouTube's Shorts lead has said nothing in the algorithm expands or shrinks reach because a channel posts more. He also warned that many low-engagement clips hurt and that there is "no magical threshold" ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/news/23/08/34020532/youtube-shorts-chief-debunks-popular-myths-no-posting-10-videos-a-day-doesnt-affect-your-performance)). The data fits. Metricool found views *per Short* peak at **2-4 uploads a week**, while monthly totals have "no real ceiling" ([Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/)). vidIQ's study of 10.2M channels shows subscriber growth rising with upload frequency up to its top bucket of 12+ a month, but it mixes Shorts and long-form and says nothing about 3 a day versus 10 ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-upload-frequency-study/)). Blog claims that more than 3 or 5 a day "triggers spam signals" cite nothing and contradict YouTube's statement ([Predis](https://predis.ai/resources/how-many-youtube-shorts-should-i-post/)).

The best clip channels win on selection. In CreatorDB's August 2026 census, Nexxo Recaps drew **111.2M views from 23 clips** in 30 days, while Kai Cenat Live posted 67 clips for 61.6M ([CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creator-news/the-rise-of-the-clip-economy/)). No verified case of a channel succeeding at 10+ Shorts a day turned up. The source material is not the limit: podcast-clipping norms are 3-6 kept Shorts per episode ([Castmagic](https://www.castmagic.io/blog/generate-short-videos-from-podcast)), and a multi-hour Kino or 404 stream produces many scored picks. The limit is the upload cap. YouTube publishes no number, only "limited" and "higher" daily limits tied to verification and channel history, and API, Studio and app uploads all count against it ([YouTube Help: feature access](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9890437)). The Data API quota is no longer the bottleneck: uploads have their own bucket of 100 `videos.insert` calls a day ([YouTube Data API](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/getting-started)). Every extra Short spends a slot from the shared 15-a-day budget that could carry a segment or compilation, the formats that earn long-form watch hours and RPM. YouTube also warns that deleting and re-uploading underperforming Shorts can look like spam ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/)), so recutting should produce genuinely different edits, not reposts.

On spacing, the evidence is thin. Metricool's sample posts most Shorts between 2 and 6 p.m. and sees engagement peak in the evening ([Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/)). The current 10:00/15:00/20:00 schedule fits that. Topical drama clips should go first while the subject is hot. Evergreen character moments can trickle out from a backlog.

## The related-video link is the only clickable bridge, and it is manual

Since 2023 YouTube has removed clickable links from Shorts descriptions and comments. What it offers instead is one "Related video" link per Short, shown as the linked video's title beneath the Short's title, pointing to any video on the same channel ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-adds-shorts-links-limits-links-elsewhere/495463)). YouTube's own guide suggests using it to route Shorts viewers to long-form or a livestream ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-related-videos-traffic-guide/)). It is set in Studio on desktop, and the API cannot set it. No official click-through rate exists. Third-party claims of 10-20% long-form lifts from systematic linking are unverified ([Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-hurting-long-form-views)), and vendor claims about pinned-comment links can be dismissed, since those links are not clickable on Shorts. vidIQ's practical advice is to link a video that directly continues the Short and to say or show a reason to tap ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-related-links-connect-shorts-long-videos/)).

This shapes the edit. If a Short delivers the whole payoff, there is less reason to click. That is an author's argument, not data ([CreatiCalc](https://creaticalc.com/blog/do-youtube-shorts-hurt-long-form)), but it fits how the formats differ. A 20-40 s Short that lands the take and the first reaction, linked to the full segment where the argument plays out, is a trailer. A 170 s Short that plays the whole bit is a substitute. Hot-take cuts suit this naturally: the take is the hook, and the segment is the payoff. YouTube's "Edit into a Short" tool does link a Short back to its source automatically, but it works only in the mobile app on the uploader's own long video ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2022/07/28/youtube-creators-can-now-turn-old-vods-into-new-shorts/)), so it does not fit an automated pipeline.

## Conclusion: what to change first, and how to know it worked

The owner's instinct is right about the ingredients (very short, hot-take-first cuts) and wrong about the dial. Volume is the weakest lever available, because the channel already posts at a volume the data associates with zero long-form lift. Each extra Short competes with segments for the same upload slots, and the policy exposure of an automated clip channel grows with its output. Length and linking are the strong levers. The current setup leaves both unused: it builds every Short in the weakest length band and in the band where one Content ID claim is fatal, and it relies on a manual link step with no prompt to do it. Also note what nobody knows. No public data covers stream-commentary clip channels specifically, Shorts-to-long-form click-through, or claim rates on stream clips. The channel's own Studio data will settle these questions faster than more outside research.

| Priority | Change | Why | Cost and effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set `[shorts] window = "short"`, `min_s` about 15, `max_s = 59`, so the default Short is the scored 15-60 s hot-take window. | Moves output from the 51-median bucket toward the 188-901 range and out of the >60 s Content ID block. | Config change and a `clipper shorts` re-render. The short window already exists, so no `score` rerun is needed. Try it on `kick-131151926` first. |
| 2 | Add a long lane as a flag on `clipper shorts`: a 60-179 s cut only for picks with a complete setup-to-payoff arc and **no** source audio, capped at about 1 in 10 Shorts. Hard rule: any Short with source audio (`allow_reactions`) is 60 s or less. | Keeps long Shorts where a story needs them while removing global-block risk. | Small code change. The scoring prompt may need a new version to flag "complete arc," per the prompt-versioning rule. |
| 3 | Make the related-video step fast. When a Short is scheduled, post its Studio edit link and the matching segment's URL to the war room or Discord, and track "related set" in Kitsu through `clipper/actions.py`. Make sure the segment uploads before or alongside its Shorts. | It is the only clickable bridge, and the API cannot set it. A one-click checklist turns a forgotten chore into 3 × 30 s a day. | Bot and Kitsu change, no model cost. |
| 4 | Edit for the hot take: open on the claim within the first second, put context in a short on-screen line naming the person, keep captions at 4-7 words, end on the reaction or a loop. Add a brief "full segment linked" cue on cuts meant as trailers. | Viewed-vs-swiped is decided in 1-3 s, and trailers convert better than complete payoffs. | A new scoring and meta prompt version. Titles name the person and the claim, never use the word "hosts", and never put profanity in titles or overlay text. |
| 5 | Hold at 3 a day while lengths change, then test 4-5 a day for two weeks, keeping Shorts at or below about half of uploads so segments keep their slots. | Change one variable at a time. Per-Short reach holds near current volume, but long-form lift fades at high Shorts volume. | Scheduler setting only. |
| 6 | Guardrails for the channel as a whole: cap Shorts per week about any one non-show person, keep slur bleeping on, and vary caption styles, layouts and title patterns so the feed does not read as templated. | Harassment pattern-of-targeting and reused/inauthentic review both judge the channel, not single videos. | Track a subject per Short in `shorts.json` or Kitsu. Small code change. |

What to measure, on Shorts posted after the length change, compared by length bucket (under 30 s, 30-60 s, over 60 s):

| Metric | Where | Decision it drives |
|---|---|---|
| Engaged views per Short (not public views) | Studio Advanced Mode | Which length bucket gets more slots. Public views are inflated by loops since March 2025. |
| Viewed vs swiped away, average percentage viewed | Studio > Content > Shorts | Whether hooks work. Creators informally aim for about 70%+ viewed; well below that means the first 1-3 s are failing. |
| Subscribers gained per 1,000 engaged views | Per-video analytics | Whether short hot-take cuts recruit as well as long ones. |
| Long-video traffic from "Shorts feed" and related-video clicks, per segment | Segment traffic sources | The funnel itself. If this stays flat while Shorts views climb, stop adding Shorts. |
| "Formats your viewers watch" | Studio > Audience | Whether the audience the Shorts build also watches long videos. |
| Content ID claims on every Short, especially any over 60 s | Studio > Copyright, or upload status | Whether the 60 s cap for Shorts with source audio must become universal. |
| Long-form watch hours toward 4,000, and RPM once monetized | Studio > Monetization | The outcome the Shorts exist to serve. |
| Upload-cap hits (`uploadLimitExceeded`) and Shorts share of uploads | Bot logs | Whether more Shorts are crowding out segments. |

Run each bucket for about 20 Shorts before drawing conclusions. If the under-30 s and 30-60 s buckets clearly beat over-60 s on engaged views and subscribers per view, and segment traffic from Shorts rises, make the shift permanent and only then try the volume test. If segment traffic from Shorts stays near zero after linking is consistent, more Shorts will not fix it.
