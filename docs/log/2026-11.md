## 2026-11-13: `clipper doc`, the mini-documentary pipeline

The first mini-documentary was built by about 145 one-off Resolve scripts, each patched from the last.
`clipper doc` is that method as a command (design agreed with the user first: the name, a TOML plan, the
leak check on Modal, and `[story] lufs` so stories can target -14 too).

- `plan.toml` replaces the hard-coded Python plan: `[[seq]]` entries (section, card, clip, say), cover
  shots cued on a line's own words, checked when the plan is read, so a bad cue fails before narration is
  bought. `say` lines sharing a `take` are one read, cut in the pause before each line.
- Word times come from a small local Whisper, aligned to the script (`doc.align`), not searched in the
  heard words: a number read out ("800") no longer breaks a cue. faster-whisper's own file reader fails
  with this PyAV, so the audio is decoded by ffmpeg, as the old scripts did.
- The layout is the old plan loop, with its special cases as plan options (`hard_out`, `mute`, `silent`,
  `sound`, `note`). One fix on the way: a take cut in two was a frame short or long at the cut, because
  the block's length and the sound's were rounded separately.
- Levels are worked out before Resolve (`set_levels`: clips and takes to `[doc] dialogue_lufs`, music and
  room tone under it), so the Resolve step only places rows, sets gains and fades, and reads back.
- `master`: gain plus a limiter, picture copied. On a 45 s excerpt of a real render: -15.9 -> -14.2 LUFS.
  The limiter sits 0.8 dB under `[doc] true_peak`: at the ceiling itself the AAC encode peaked 0.6 dB over.
- `leak`: banned terms against the render's Modal transcript and kit.md; exact, joined or split, and
  close spellings (`[doc] leak_fuzzy`); `!phrase` in banned.txt marks a false alarm.
- Tried on real files (free): take processing (17.0 -> 15.9 s), word timing (96% of script words
  matched), the split, mastering. Not tried: Resolve (a stand-in in `tests/test_doc_resolve.py`),
  ElevenLabs, Modal. 759 tests pass.
