## The episode skeleton (1997, about 23 min)

Every episode works the same way. Long set pieces of 3 to 6 minutes are separated by palette
cleansers of about 60 seconds, and a single thread runs through and escalates.

1. **Cold open sketch** (1-1.5 min): grave narration over something absurd, which ends on the
   episode's question ("are we too nice or too nasty?").
2. **Titles.**
3. **Anchor monologue** with stat gags and fake headlines (1.5-2.5 min).
4. **Filmed report #1** (3-5 min): mock-gravitas voice-over, talking heads and a doorstep.
5. **The thread, part 1** (3-5 min): a fake campaign, cause, drug or crisis with an invented
   name and jargon.
6. **Palette cleanser** (about 1 min): a fake ad, a fake archive clip, a graphic gag or a
   quick-fire verdict round.
7. **Filmed report #2** or a studio debate (3-5 min).
8. **The thread, part 2: escalation** (2-6 min): the thing gets bigger, sillier and more official.
9. **Wrap-up** (1-3 min): a recap that makes it worse, callbacks and a sign-off question.
   Sometimes a post-credits stinger follows.

**The 2001 special differs.** It covers one story for the whole half hour. The show mobilises the
nation, its live reporter returns four times while the scene escalates, the set gets invaded, and
the wrap-up congratulates the show on a result it caused. It is the best model for a week where
one drama dominates.

## Devices, and what each one becomes for us

| Device (where it is strongest) | The Udder Truth version |
|---|---|
| **Invented jargon and acronym charities** ("Czech neck", "heavy electricity", the long-acronym elephant charity; eps 1-3) | An on-screen glossary of nonsense "chat codes" and an official-sounding body with an unpronounceable acronym. Mother Moo says them with total sincerity. |
| **The escalating thread** (the elephant, cake, heavy electricity) | One invented crisis per week, tied to the real week's drama, with a part 1 in the first half and part 2 near the end that is bigger and more official. |
| **The failing live cross** (the garage in ep 6, the prison in 2001) | "Live outside the streamer's house": an original reporter character returns 3 to 5 times. Nothing happens each time, the reporting gets more breathless, and the last visit pays off. This is cheap to make: one generated reporter and one plate. |
| **The verdict refrain** ("right or wrong", "innocent or guilty"; eps 1-3) | A refrain of Mother Moo's own that closes each item, with a scorecard graphic. The words are to be written; don't copy theirs. |
| **Absurd statistic and gauge graphics** (the "modelometer", "mad-ometer", the cruelty/intelligence chart) | A weekly gauge graphic built once in Fusion (a "Cow-ometer", for example), with real numbers framed absurdly: chat-heat peaks, viewer counts, donation totals from our own data. |
| **Mock-gravitas narration** (every report) | Mother Moo's voice-over on real clips, played straight, in purple prose. The real people's words stay exactly what they said. |
| **Fake ads and public-information films** (eps 3 and 5) | These are the magazine's parody ads (#58), cut as 45 to 60 s TV spots. A good/bad pair works as a palette cleanser. |
| **The anchor bullying a guest** (eps 1 and 4) | Mother Moo interrogates an original fake expert or "concerned viewer" at the desk. That person is never a real one. |
| **The show causing the panic** (2001) | Mother Moo launches a "watch" or hotline and proudly reports the tips that her own viewers sent in. The wrap-up credits the show with saving everyone. |
| **The made-up reveal, then carrying on** ("cake is a made-up drug"; ep 2) | Halfway through, admit the crisis is invented, then escalate anyway. |
| **Callbacks and the post-credits stinger** | One line from the cold open returns in the wrap-up, plus a 5-second stinger after the end card (good for Shorts). |

## What we leave behind

- **Celebrity hoax interviews and celebrity PSAs** (in every episode). They only work by duping
  real people, so we replace them with original characters. Real people appear only in real
  clips, saying what they said.
- **Doorsteps of real people.** We doorstep original characters or fake institutions only.
- **The subject matter** of eps 4 to 6 and 2001 (real killers, abuse, race, AIDS). We take the
  shape and leave the content.
- **Footage, jokes, music, emblem and catchphrases.** We use rhythm, structure and attitude only.

## Studio look (from 20 frames, 2026-10-24)

Twenty studio frames from the same upload, grabbed from a temporary 360p copy (deleted afterwards),
are in `refs/brass_eye/` (gitignored, private reference; the file name is the time in the video).
They are for describing qualities only: never pass them to an image model, and never copy the set,
emblem or on-screen text.

- **Dark, not bright.** The sets are mostly black. The presenter gets hard front light and the
  background falls away into pools of colour. Nothing like an evenly lit newsroom
  (0-26-40, 1-12-50, 2-37-20).
- **Blue against orange.** Deep navy and electric blue throughout, cut with hot amber and flame
  gradients on bezels, floors and graphics, with purple and red neon as accents (0-16-10, 0-53-50,
  1-30-30).
- **Every screen is alive.** Stacks of CRTs flank the presenter, showing the show's emblem, colour
  bars or the story's footage; there is a gallery wall of dozens of monitors with a LIVE bug; big
  projected images stand beside him. No screen is ever black (0-46-20, 1-47-50, 2-05-00, 2-20-40).
- **The desk is a small island in a big void.** In the wides, a compact desk with the emblem lit on
  its front panel sits in a huge dark space over a glowing red-orange floor grid (0-53-50, 2-17-10).
- **Neon slogan boards.** Red and blue lit panels with sensational slogans surround the desk
  (1-12-50, 1-30-30): a recurring gag we can do with our own text in Fusion.
- **Interview set.** Guest in a dark leather chair against a starfield-blue backdrop with a
  vertical blue light column and a chrome ribbon mic on the desk (1-15-40, 1-41-00, 2-17-00). Guests
  are also shown "inside" an orange-bezelled monitor frame.
- **Debate set.** Blue floor, tan geometric flats, a low round table and armchairs (0-07-00).
- **Graphic boards.** The presenter stands beside a big flat graphic (a diagram, a scale) as a
  physical set piece (0-34-30).
- **2001 special.** Cooler and darker, like a control room: the presenter stands in front of banks of
  small screens, and a two-handed desk has a large flat screen behind it (2-37-20, 2-40-50, 2-46-20).
- **Camera.** Mid-shots and close-ups, centred, often a touch low, with slow push-ins; the wides
  make the presenter small in a huge space.
