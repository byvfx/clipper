# Shorts posting volume and cadence for a new clip channel (Lolcow Cinema)

Labels used below: **[Official]** = YouTube/Google staff or docs; **[3P data]** = third-party dataset with a stated sample; **[3P opinion]** = blog/vendor advice without data; **[Anecdote]** = single-case or marketing claim. Research date: 2026-10-21.

## 1. Does posting more Shorts per day help or hurt per-Short reach? (frequency, spacing, time of day, cross-Short effects)

### Takeaway
YouTube's position is that the algorithm does not raise or lower a channel's reach based on how many Shorts it posts. It ranks each video on how viewers respond to it, so volume hurts only when it brings weaker clips. Third-party data agrees: views *per Short* peak at a few per week and dip slightly at higher volume, but *total* monthly views and subscriber growth keep rising with volume. No source gives a measured penalty threshold, such as "more than N per day."

### Cited Findings
- [Official, Aug 2023, older] YouTube Shorts product lead Todd Sherman: nothing in the algorithm expands or reduces reach because you post more Shorts. He warned that many low-quality, low-engagement clips can hurt, and said there is "no magical threshold" of posts. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-advice-for-shorts-creators-hashtags-algorithm/691835/); [Benzinga: "Posting 10 Videos A Day Doesn't Affect Your Performance"](https://www.benzinga.com/news/23/08/34020532/youtube-shorts-chief-debunks-popular-myths-no-posting-10-videos-a-day-doesnt-affect-your-performance); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/)
- [Official, Aug 2023] Sherman advised against deleting and re-uploading Shorts that underperform, because YouTube may treat that as spam. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/)
- [Official, quoted second-hand] Rene Ritchie (YouTube creator liaison), as quoted: "We don't look at channel or creator for those sorts of things. We look at *video* and *topic*." This means a weak Short should not suppress the next upload. The quote reaches us through third-party blogs, so the exact wording is unverified. — [ytgrowth.io](https://ytgrowth.io/blog/shorts-vs-long-form); [subscribr.ai](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-separate-or-main-channel)
- [3P data, Metricool, July 2026, 799,718 videos / 71,177 accounts] "Each Short performs best per post at 2 to 4 uploads a week." For monthly totals there is "no real ceiling," and monthly views rise with more uploads. A secondary summary of the same study gives per-post views of about 5,872 at 2-4/week and about 5,157 at 1-2/week, lower at the extremes. Metricool did not report a daily-or-more bucket. — [Metricool YouTube Trends 2026](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/) (per-post figures via the WebSearch summary of that page; the page's chart images did not render)
- [3P data, Metricool 2026] The study also looked at long-form performance by Shorts cadence. Channels posting about 5 Shorts/month averaged 8,789 views per long-form video (about 46% above the 5,985 global average). Channels posting 35+ Shorts/month were "roughly the same level as channels posting none" (4,364). This is correlation only. — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/)
- [3P data, vidIQ, July 2026, 10.2M channels, Shorts and long-form combined] Median subscriber growth rises at every upload tier. Channels posting 12+ uploads/month grew a median 0.91%/month, with 4.1x the view growth of the baseline. An earlier vidIQ study (5.08M channels, June 2024 to June 2025) found 12+/month channels grew subscribers over 3x faster than channels posting less than monthly. Neither study separates Shorts, and the top bucket is "12+", so neither says anything about 5 versus 20 per day. — [vidIQ upload frequency study](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-upload-frequency-study/); [vidIQ blog](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/How-Often-Post-on-Youtube)
- [3P opinion] Blogs recommend anything from 1-3 Shorts/day to 1-5/day. Some claim more than 3/day "dilutes" performance, and one claims more than 5/day "triggers spam signals." None cites data, and these claims contradict Sherman. — [StoryClips](https://www.storyclips.ai/blog/how-many-youtube-shorts-should-i-post-a-day); [Predis](https://predis.ai/resources/how-many-youtube-shorts-should-i-post/); [Virlo](https://virlo.ai/blog/how-often-to-post-youtube-shorts); [Social Champ](https://www.socialchamp.com/blog/how-often-should-you-post-youtube-shorts/)
- [3P opinion] Buffer's general guide suggests 1-3 Shorts per week. Buffer's own frequency data covers TikTok, not Shorts: 2-5 TikToks/week earned up to 17% more views per post than weekly posting. — [Buffer frequency guide](https://buffer.com/resources/social-media-frequency-guide/)
- [3P data, time of day] In Metricool's sample, most Shorts go up between 2 and 6 p.m., and "engagement peaks in the evening." For YouTube content generally, videos usually peak in the first three days, and about 83% of interactions arrive within ten days. — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/)
- [3P opinion] Weekend and best-time claims, such as "weekends get 60% more," come from vendor blogs with unclear methods. — [PostEverywhere](https://posteverywhere.ai/blog/best-time-to-schedule-youtube-shorts)

### Inferences
- For a clip channel, the limit on volume is clip quality, not an algorithmic quota. The real risk at high volume is padding the feed with weak clips. Each weak clip has its own poor test performance, and taken together they raise reused-content and repetitive-content flags (Section 4).
- Since per-Short reach dips only slightly while totals keep growing, moving from 1/day to about 3-6/day should raise total views, provided each clip passes the same quality bar. Spacing them a few hours apart, at evenings or 2-6 p.m. in the audience's timezone, is reasonable but weakly evidenced.
- The Metricool finding that "35+ Shorts/month ≈ no lift for long-form" hints that heavy Shorts volume may not funnel viewers into long-form. It is correlation only, but worth watching in the channel's own analytics.

### Gaps
- I found no Creator Insider video or Help Center article that states a recommended number of Shorts per day. The official statements found are the 2023 Sherman Q&A and second-hand Ritchie quotes.
- No dataset reports per-Short performance for channels posting 5+, 10+ or 20+ Shorts/day.
- No official statement on whether Shorts posted close together compete for the same subscriber feed slots.

## 2. Long tail vs short burst: implications for posting a backlog

### Takeaway
Shorts mostly get their views in the first days, but some later "find a seed audience" and spike again. Rigorous data on Shorts resurfacing weeks later does not exist publicly. A backlog can be posted gradually without much lost value, because stream clips age slowly except for topical drama.

### Cited Findings
- [Official, 2023] Sherman: the algorithm sometimes finds a "seed audience" for a Short, after which "it may get a lot more traffic or it may taper off." — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/)
- [3P data] For YouTube content generally, peak is usually in the first 3 days, and about 83% of interactions come in the first 10 days. This is not Shorts-specific. — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/)
- [3P data, Metricool 2026] Average view duration per Short fell about 67% to roughly 16 seconds, while total Shorts views grew 127% year over year. — via [Metricool YouTube Trends](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/) / [Tubefilter coverage](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/03/youtube-shorts-long-form-study-metricool/)
- [Academic, 2024] A comparison of Shorts and regular videos found that mean views of regular videos declined slowly and steadily with age, while mean views of Shorts *fluctuated*. That is consistent with irregular resurfacing. — [ACM WebSci 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3614419.3644023)
- [3P opinion] Blogs say Shorts can resurface weeks or months later, with search the main source of long-tail Shorts views. No measured data. — [Miraflow](https://miraflow.ai/blog/what-happens-youtube-shorts-after-30-days-old-content-views); [Hookclone](https://www.hookclone.com/blog/how-many-views-on-youtube-shorts-is-good)
- [3P data, long-form only] Of 367 long-form videos at least 90 days old, 47.7% were gaining views faster in the second half of the tracking window. This does not apply directly to Shorts. — [OverseerOS](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/can-youtube-video-go-viral-months-later)

### Inferences
- Post time-sensitive drama clips first, while the topic is hot. Evergreen character moments and running jokes can trickle out from a backlog at the daily cap without losing much value.
- Searchable titles (names, the incident) matter for any long tail, since search is the most plausible source of late views.
- Do not delete and repost underperformers (Sherman: may be treated as spam). Recutting a clip into a genuinely different edit is a different matter.

### Gaps
- No public dataset on Shorts view curves past 30 days.

## 3. Upload limits, API quota, and API vs app uploads

### Takeaway
The binding constraint is YouTube's per-channel daily upload limit. YouTube does not publish the number; it depends on verification and channel history. The Data API quota is not the bottleneck: since June 2026 uploads have their own bucket of 100 `videos.insert` calls/day, at about 1 unit each. As far as public sources show, API uploads count against the same channel limit as Studio/app uploads.

### Cited Findings
- [Official] YouTube's feature-access table lists a "Limited daily limit" for uploads with no verification, and a "Higher daily limit" with phone verification or with channel history / ID verification. Features also depend on following Community Guidelines and keeping a positive channel history. No numbers are published. — [YouTube Help: feature access](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9890437)
- [3P] The exact daily limit isn't published and "varies depending on your channel's status and behavior." Once hit, uploads are blocked for about 24 hours with no override. — [Riverside help](https://support.riverside.com/hc/en-us/articles/26295750508189); [Viraly](https://viraly.io/docs/youve-reached-your-youtube-upload-limit-what-it-means-and-what-to-do)
- [3P] Desktop, mobile and API uploads count toward the same channel-level limit, and switching OAuth apps does not bypass it. — [Composio KB](https://docs.composio.dev/kb/guide/toolkits-youtube) (secondary; consistent with the project's own observation that `uploadLimitExceeded` is separate from quota)
- [Official] Data API default allocation: "100 `search.list` calls, 100 `videos.insert` calls, and 10,000 units per day combined for all other endpoints"; "A video upload costs 1 unit." The allocation is "subject to change." — [YouTube Data API getting started](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/getting-started)
- [Official] Revision history, Dec 4, 2025: upload quota cost changed "from approximately 1600 units to approximately 100 units." Revision history, June 1, 2026: "videos.insert and search.list methods will be charged to their own respective quota buckets." The old 1,600-unit figure (about 6 uploads/day on 10k units) is obsolete. — [YouTube Data API revision history](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/revision_history)
- [Official, July 2020, older] Videos uploaded via `videos.insert` from *unverified* API projects created after July 28, 2020 "will be restricted to private viewing mode" until the project passes an audit. — [Revision history](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/revision_history)

### Inferences
- With 100 upload calls/day available, quota allows far more uploads than the channel limit. To post more, the levers are channel verification (phone, then ID/video verification for advanced features) and channel age and clean history. A more efficient API path does not help.
- Count every upload toward the cap: long segments, compilations, short clips, preshow and Shorts all share it. Raising Shorts volume means fewer other uploads, or waiting for the cap to grow.
- Repeatedly hitting `uploadLimitExceeded` is likely harmless, but YouTube says feature access depends on "behavior" and history, so a scheduler that retries hard against the cap is not ideal. This is an inference; no source confirms a penalty.
- I found no evidence that YouTube treats Shorts uploaded via the API differently in distribution from Studio/app uploads. A Short is classified by its vertical/square aspect ratio and duration, not by upload path.

### Gaps
- No published numeric daily upload cap for new, phone-verified or ID-verified channels. The project's observed ~8-15/24h is consistent with "limited" but cannot be checked against a source.
- No official statement on API vs app upload distribution parity. Only absence of evidence.
- I could not confirm the post-June-2026 quota figures beyond the Google pages fetched; the Cloud Console Quotas page is authoritative for this project.

## 4. Reused / repetitive ("inauthentic") content policy risk for a clip channel

### Takeaway
This is the biggest risk of high-volume clip Shorts. YouTube's reused-content policy applies to the channel as a whole, applies even with the original creator's permission, and explicitly lists "clips from a show edited together with little narrative" and promoting others' content as ineligible. Clips with a storyline, commentary, or "meaningful difference" from the original are allowed. The July 2025 update renamed "repetitious" to "inauthentic content" to cover mass-produced, templated output. The Help page now titles that section "Generic or Repetitive Content." A pipeline mass-producing near-identical templated Shorts is the profile both policies target.

### Cited Findings
- [Official, Help Center, current page] **Reused content, not allowed:** clips from a show edited together with little narrative; short compilations from other social sites; content uploaded many times by different creators; promoting others' content even with permission; downloaded or copied content without substantive changes; reaction content with mostly non-verbal reactions and no voice commentary; readings of others' material. **Allowed:** clips used in critical reviews; reaction videos that comment on the original; edited footage with a storyline and commentary; Shorts remixes that add original content; content mainly featuring the uploader. Viewers must be able to see a "meaningful difference" from the original. — [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- [Official] **Enforcement:** the policy "applies to your channel as a whole." Violating videos, or content where authorship is unclear, can remove monetization from the entire channel. Reviewers look at videos, the channel description, titles and descriptions. It is not copyright-based: it applies even with the original creator's permission, and a channel can violate it with zero copyright claims. It applies to Shorts in addition to the Shorts monetization policies. — [same page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- [Official] **Generic or repetitive content** (formerly repetitious; July 15, 2025 update renamed it "inauthentic content" and said it covers repetitive or mass-produced content): not allowed are similar content with low educational value or minimal variation, recurring setups with the same outcome, templated storylines, and "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates." Allowed: the same intro/outro if the main content differs, and series where each video has a distinct focus. — [same page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- [Official, via press] Rene Ritchie called the July 2025 change a "minor update" to long-standing YPP policies, not aimed at reaction/commentary videos. Automated tools and templates are allowed if the result shows creative vision and value. — [TubeBuddy](https://www.tubebuddy.com/blog/youtube-monetization-update/); [Exemplary](https://exemplary.ai/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy); [Async](https://async.com/blog/youtube-monetization-update)
- [3P, unconfirmed] Two 2026 blogs say YouTube renamed the policy again in July 2026 to "Generic or Repetitive Content" and split it into named categories without changing the rules. The live Help page heading, "Generic or Repetitive Content," is consistent with this, but I found no YouTube announcement. — [Audit Socials](https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-2026-mass-produced-ai-generated-monetization-creators-brands); [ytzolo](https://ytzolo.com/blog/youtube-repetitive-content-policy-ai-slop-2026/)
- [3P] YPP thresholds (2023 change, older): 500 subscribers, 3 public uploads in 90 days, and 3,000 watch hours/year or 3M Shorts views in 90 days for the lower tier. — [TechCrunch 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/06/13/youtube-is-lowering-the-barrier-to-be-eligible-for-its-monetization-program/)
- [Anecdote/marketing] Sellers of "reused content appeal" services claim thousands of compilation and faceless channels are denied YPP for reused content. This is marketing copy with no independent data. — [Gumroad listing](https://creatorinside.gumroad.com/l/ReusedContentAppealService)
- [3P data] Large streamer-clip channels operate at scale. Kai Cenat Live has 16.3M subscribers and DaPlug 1.6M, and CreatorDB (Aug 2026) records no enforcement actions against them. CreatorDB does not discuss policy, so this shows only that the business model can survive, not that it is safe. — [CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creator-news/the-rise-of-the-clip-economy/)

### Inferences
- Permission from Kino Casino / Channel 404 (the project notes Don gave permission) protects against copyright claims, **not** against reused-content rulings, per the Help page.
- Features that move a stream clip toward "meaningful difference": a storyline across cuts (setup, payoff), captions that add context, on-screen framing or commentary text, compilation themes with a narrative, and titles and descriptions that explain the moment. The current pipeline's long segments and compilations with editorial structure are the stronger YPP evidence. Bare 30-60 s raw cuts posted en masse are the weakest.
- Reviewers judge the channel as a whole, including titles and descriptions. A feed of many near-identical templated Shorts (same title pattern, same thumbnail text, same framing) is exactly the "minimal variation / templated" pattern, so vary the templates and keep human editorial picks visible.
- The YPP review happens when the channel applies (at 500 subs / 3M Shorts views or 1,000 / 10M for full ad revenue). High Shorts volume before that review piles up the evidence a reviewer will look at, so quality per Short matters more than count.

### Gaps
- No public data on YPP approval rates for streamer-clip channels, or on how reviewers weigh Shorts against long-form in a mixed channel.
- No official example specific to "clips of a livestream with the streamer's permission."

## 5. One channel vs a separate Shorts channel (2024-2026)

### Takeaway
YouTube staff guidance, quoted second-hand, is "same audience, same channel." They say Shorts do not drag down long-form because videos are judged individually. The only data point against heavy Shorts on a long-form channel is Metricool's correlation that very high Shorts volume (35+/month) coincides with no long-form lift. A clips operation for a different audience is a commonly cited reason to split.

### Cited Findings
- [Official, quoted second-hand] Todd Beaupré (YouTube Director of Discovery), as quoted: "Same audience? Same channel. Different audience? Different channel." Pierce Vollucci (YouTube PM) reportedly gave similar guidance. — [subscribr.ai](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-separate-or-main-channel); [ytgrowth.io](https://ytgrowth.io/blog/shorts-vs-long-form)
- [Official, quoted second-hand] Ritchie: a Short that performs well makes viewers recognize you in the next long-form video. Shorts do not hurt long-form because they are judged per video and topic. — [ytgrowth.io](https://ytgrowth.io/blog/shorts-vs-long-form); [Panda Video](https://www.pandavideo.com/blog/shorts-and-long-form-videos-same-channel)
- [Official, 2023] YouTube said Shorts was designed to "complement, not compete with" other formats, and added links from Shorts to a creator's long-form videos. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2023/09/04/youtube-shorts-cannibalize-long-videos/)
- [Academic, 2024] Channels that started publishing Shorts saw a mean decrease in long-form views per channel. — [arXiv 2402.18208](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18208v2)
- [3P data, 2026] Long-form views were highest at about 5 Shorts/month (+46% vs average), and at 35+ Shorts/month no better than channels with no Shorts. Correlation only. — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/)

### Inferences
- Lolcow Cinema's long-form and Shorts serve the same audience (fans of the same shows), and the channel is new. Splitting would halve channel signals and split the per-channel upload cap only if YouTube grants each channel its own limit (likely, but unverified). A second channel *would* be a way around the daily upload cap, but it starts from zero history with its own "limited" upload tier and its own YPP review.
- Watch the evidence of possible long-form suppression at very high Shorts volume (arXiv, Metricool) in the channel's own analytics before moving to 35+/month.

### Gaps
- No primary-source video quote for the Beaupré and Ritchie lines in this session (all second-hand).
- No controlled data on split vs combined channels for clip operations.

## 6. Examples of clip channels growing via high-volume Shorts

### Takeaway
The large streamer-clip channels post about 1-2 clips a day *per streamer* on average, not dozens. Their scale comes from years of multiple daily uploads plus the streamer's fanbase and paid clipping campaigns. I found no verified case of a channel succeeding with 10+ Shorts/day.

### Cited Findings
- [3P data, CreatorDB, Aug 26, 2026, 30-day window, clips of six tracked streamers only] Nexxo Recaps: 355K subs, 23 clips/30d, 111.2M views, +200% subs over 90 days. deagzzzshorts: 6.5M subs, 8 clips, 91.8M views. Stream Bellz: 289K, 48 clips, 74.9M. DaPlug: 1.6M, 53 clips, 71.6M. Stream Vortex: 366K, 32 clips, 69.7M, +63%. Goofy Recaps: 3.6M, 29 clips, 63.2M. Snickerly: 1.4M, 49 clips, 61.7M. Kai Cenat Live: 16.3M, 67 clips, 61.6M. Lifetime totals: Snickerly 3,771 videos, DaPlug 3,365, described as "multiple uploads per day." — [CreatorDB: The Clip Economy](https://creatordb.app/creator-news/the-rise-of-the-clip-economy/)
- [3P data] Paid clipping rates: Whop campaigns $0.50-$1.50 per 1K views (range $0.20-$6); Vyro (MrBeast) $3/1K; N3on pays $40-50 per 100K views. Clips are not labelled as paid distribution on any platform. — [CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creator-news/the-rise-of-the-clip-economy/)
- [Anecdote, vendor] Three small gaming creators: follower growth tracked posting volume more than virality. The top gainer posted 29 clips and went from 47 to 1,710 followers, with a best clip of 5,900 views. — [Eklipse](https://blog.eklipse.gg/streaming-tips/content-creation/grow-streaming-channel-clips.html)
- [Anecdote, vendor, unverified] A business podcaster supposedly went from 5K to 250K followers posting 20 clips/day across TikTok, Reels, Shorts and X. — via [ClipFarming case studies](https://clipfarming.org/resources/case-studies) / clipping-service marketing; treat as unreliable.
- [3P opinion] 1-3 Shorts/day is described as the common clip-channel cadence. Start at 1/day and go to 2-3/day once the channel is established. — [Conbersa](https://www.conbersa.ai/learn/podcast-clip-youtube-shorts-algorithm); [TokPortal](https://www.tokportal.com/use-cases/youtube-shorts-channel-warming-clipping-networks)

### Inferences
- The best-performing clip channels in the census (Nexxo Recaps: 23 clips → 111M views) win on clip selection, not volume. For Lolcow Cinema, going from 1/day to a few per day of the best moments is in line with the top channels' observed per-streamer cadence. Going to 10+/day is not supported by any example found, and would run into the upload cap anyway.

### Gaps
- No verified per-day Shorts counts (all uploads) for the named clip channels, and no data on their YPP status or revenue splits.
- No case studies specific to commentary/drama-stream clip channels (as opposed to IRL/gaming streamer clips or podcasts).
